E-learning

Although access to Ozempic is Yazen’s most well-known selling point, the treatment actually encompasses a broad range of measures. One that I played a key role in implementing was the mobile app’s e-learning functionality. This involved defining a CMS schema for several modules and chapters of multimedia content and tracking progress in Firebase. During my tenure we took this from designs to a mature production feature in daily use by thousands of patients.

Managing thousands of pages of content across multiple languages was operationally complex for a small team and I improvised many small tools integrating services such as ChatGPT to streamline the work.

Nutrition tracking

Not all weight loss patients want to track their diets, but for those who do, we added a nutrition tracking feature during my time at Yazen. It proved an uncommonly interesting problem space.

Ingesting a large third-party dataset about retail food products was a memorable early challenge. Building a barcode scanner was a uniquely weird task that saw several items from our kitchen cupboard migrate to my work desk for a while in order to have things close to hand for test scanning. And meeting user expectations about daily calorie counts involved answering difficult philosophical questions about the socially constructed nature of time when considering meals registered in different timezones while travelling.

Localisation

The weight input UX is of critical importance for a service like Yazen. Their expansion into the UK provided an opportunity to share some local knowledge with the team, who were understandably surprised to learn about the British use of the stone as a unit of body weight. I then implemented an imperial/metric units switcher to localise this crucial component at the very top of the company’s conversion funnel.
