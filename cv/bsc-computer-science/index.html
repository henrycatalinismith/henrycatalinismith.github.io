First class honours in a degree I was originally underqualified to study for.

Transferring to Computer Science

As I hadn’t done mathematics at A-level I wasn’t qualified for the flagship BSc Computer Science course. For my first semester I was enrolled in a BSc Software Engineering course that started off the same, but would have less emphasis on math-heavy topics like computational complexity in later semesters. This was a problem for me, as that was the stuff I was most excited to get into.

So I studied my absolute pants off in that first semester and pulled off a grade of 94% in the Foundations Of Computer Science module. Evidently this was enough to persuade the right people in the Department of Computer Science to let me shoot my shot in the harder course.

Internship

The more vocational software engineering course I began this degree on required students to do an internship for a work-based learning module. By the time I successfully negotiated a transfer to the more academic computer science course at the end of the first semester, I’d already set up a placement at a local charity called the Civic Trust.

This was a really formative year for me. I was helping with front- and back-end development (it was all just called “web development” back then by the way) on several sites, as well as developing data importing tools that saved the charity a decent bit of money.
