Certified ScrumMaster

Valid from 2014-08 to 2016-08

Paid out of my own pocket for a two day course with a perfunctory exam which taught me some unexpected lessons about myself.

Expected Outcome

I was young and naive. Not only did I pay for this course personally, but I also used annual leave to attend. I’d recently been promoted into a team lead role, the impostor syndrome was eating away at me, and this was a way to do something about it.

The team at work was really engaged and my goal with the course was to pick up some insights that would help us tweak how we structured our planning and unleash more of their potential. That, and maybe to shore up some extra credibility and/or self-confidence in the new leadership role.

Actual Outcome

As the two day course went by with all its workshops, I got plenty of chances to get to know the other attendees. Chatting with them, it dawned on me that I was the only person there using their holiday allowance and personal money to attend, and that I had done quite a weird thing.

This led to a series of follow-on realisations. It gave me a new perspective on what it can look like when people are proactive about developing their skills and have an ongoing dialog with their employer about it. It’s great to be so committed to your own development that you’ll go to the lengths I did, but it’s even better to be committed enough to involve your employer in the effort. I knew I’d internalised this lesson for sure when I got Spotify to pay for my CPWA certification eight years later.
