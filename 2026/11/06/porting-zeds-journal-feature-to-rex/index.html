Zed’s killer feature IMO is the built-in journal. You run the journal::NewJournalEntry command in the command palette (or map it to a keyboard shortcut) and it dumps you in ~/journal/YYYY/MM/DD.md ready to make a quick note about something. I recently became very excited indeed about Rex from Superlogical but wanted to bring this piece of Zed magic with me.

In exchange for about five minutes of propting I cooked up journal.lua in my ~/.config.rex directory. Pressing ⌘^J opens a little text input field that stores your input in ~/journal/YYYY/MM/DD.md when you’re ready. This actually felt like a UX improvemnent on Zed because it makes the operation feel more append-only. In Zed’s journal I would become distracted by the sight of my older entries when adding a new one whereas now I don’t even need to see them until I want to.

Even better though was once Rex’s incredible API started to sink in for me and it hit me that I can store “screenshots” to the journal too. So I hooked up another shortcut at ⌘^3, which reads the full text of the currently active pane and stores that as a journal entry in its own right. So I can grab the full UI state of even a TUI program (which are often a pain in the ass to copypaste from normally) and chuck it effortlessly into my permanent record.
